## 8. For the accountant — the business question

**This is the most important open item in the entire review.**

A resale or trading activity is clearly visible in the accounts, and the
household has confirmed a business exists. What cannot be determined from
banking data is **whether it makes money**.

What the accounts show:

- **$54,213 of inbound e-transfers over 18 months** (~$3,012/month), described
  as proceeds from selling items — but this is **gross receipts, not profit**.
- **$23,200 of outbound e-transfers** over the same period, $13,062 of it drawn
  **directly from the HELOC at 4.90%**. If these are inventory purchases, the
  business is running on borrowed working capital.
- **Heavy retail volume** — 152 Amazon Marketplace transactions plus Costco,
  Walmart, Winners, Marks, Sport Chek. All of it is currently classified as
  household spending. **Some proportion may be cost of goods.**
- **A CRA balance paid from the HELOC.** Tax owed, settled with borrowed money.
- **The HELOC grows despite large repayments**, which is what working-capital
  financing looks like when receipts do not cover outlays.

Questions an accountant would need answered:

1. Is the business registered, and in whose name? Sole proprietorship,
   partnership, or corporation?
2. Are business receipts and expenses currently separated from household
   accounts? On the evidence, **no** — they run through the same chequing
   accounts and the same HELOC.
3. What is the true cost of goods, and therefore the actual margin?
4. Is the activity at a scale that triggers **GST/HST registration**?
5. Has business income been reported, and what is the outstanding CRA position?
6. **Is any portion of the HELOC interest deductible** as business borrowing?
   At $9,878/year of interest this is material — but deductibility normally
   depends on tracing borrowed funds to business use, and the current commingling
   makes tracing difficult.
7. Does the wife's business change any of the above, and should the two
   activities be considered together?

**The single highest-value structural change available: run the business through
its own account.** It makes margin visible, makes interest traceable, and makes
the tax position defensible. It costs almost nothing to do.
