### Comparing payoff approaches — general principles, not a recommendation

- **Highest-interest-first** minimizes total interest paid. On the evidence
  available, the credit card is almost certainly your most expensive debt at an
  estimated ~24%, versus a mortgage and HELOC that are secured and will be far
  cheaper. Clearing $1,799.97 would save roughly $450/year and restore $2,000
  of emergency credit capacity.
- **Smallest-balance-first** produces faster psychological wins. Here the two
  approaches point at the same target, because the card is both the most
  expensive and the smallest TD debt. That is an unusually clean case.
- **Cash-flow stabilization first** argues for fixing the overdraft before
  attacking any balance, because NSF and overdraft charges are a recurring,
  fully avoidable leak and the overdraft is a live risk of missed payments.
- **HELOC versus mortgage prepayment** cannot be assessed. I do not have either
  rate, the HELOC's payment structure, the mortgage's prepayment privileges, or
  the renewal date. Any comparison would be guesswork.
- **Credit-card repayment versus emergency savings** is genuinely contested
  ground. Clearing a ~24% card is a guaranteed ~24% return; holding cash is
  insurance against the next NSF event. Your situation argues for doing a
  little of both rather than either exclusively — see §13.

### Before any mortgage or HELOC action

Do not act on prepayment, refinancing, consolidation, or a balance transfer
without first establishing: both interest rates; annual prepayment privileges
and the penalty formula; the mortgage renewal date; any fees; the tax treatment
of HELOC interest if any portion relates to the resale activity; and whether the
move would increase your risk by converting unsecured debt into debt secured
against your home. **That last point matters most.** Moving credit-card debt onto
a HELOC lowers the rate but puts your house behind it.

**These are decisions for a licensed mortgage professional or financial advisor,
and for TD directly. I am not one and this section is not advice.**
